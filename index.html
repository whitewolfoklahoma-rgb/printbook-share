How PrintBook pricing works

Rate card. Your customer price starts from your own numbers: blanks marked up by your blank markup %, screen-print charges from your price table (by quantity break and colors per location), a setup/screen fee for every screen (one per color, per location), embroidery priced per 1,000 stitches with a per-piece minimum, plus a one-time digitizing fee per embroidered design.

True cost. Separately, PrintBook computes what the job actually costs you: blank cost, ink/consumables per print, labor (setup minutes per screen + run time) at your labor rate, plus your overhead %.

Three prices. Break-even = true cost. The margin price = true cost ÷ (1 − target margin). Recommended is the higher of your rate card and the margin price — so a heavy setup on a small run can't sneak in underpriced. Premium = recommended + your premium uplift, for rush work.

Profit floor. If the recommended price falls below the price that delivers your floor margin, PrintBook warns you and won't save the quote unless you deliberately tick the override.

Job book. Save a quote, mark it booked when the customer approves, record deposit and payments, then complete it with the actual blank spend and hours — PrintBook shows the true profit on the job.

Honest limits

PrintBook is your shop's quoting and job book. It does not take online orders or payments, has no customer portal, and doesn't sync between devices — it's one file, on your device, with your data in your browser. Export a backup from Settings before switching devices, and before clearing browser data. Prices are estimates from your rate card — your scale tickets, supplier invoices, and press are the final truth.
